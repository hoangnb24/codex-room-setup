### Product outcome displaced by component closure ceremony

- Observation: SAMPipe repeatedly reached reviewer ACCEPT, green suites, frozen
  release, exact identity, attestation, and `INTEGRATION_COMPLETE` states while
  the original Personal Pet journey still stopped before Gate-B preview,
  Gate-B decision, installation, visible interaction, and offline restore.
- Evidence: The 2026-08-09 workflow audit counted 76 agents in roughly 36 hours
  (20 Lead, 56 Peer) around one prototype journey. Multiple component closures
  were followed by manual discovery of missing real runtime routes, client state
  mappings, provider normalization, recovery behavior, and preview forwarding.
  The final audited stopping point still had a reviewable backend Gate-B
  candidate but no Calm Gate-B display or Human decision.
- Counterevidence: Exact candidate review, durable identities, paid-attempt
  freezing, and bounded recovery prevented duplicate spend and state corruption.
  Independent reviewers found real defects; the issue is proportionality and
  placement, not the existence of safety or review.
- Suspected mechanism: No single emulator-level Definition of Done meant each
  service optimized its own acceptance boundary. Green local proof triggered
  more release/review ceremony, while child completion behaved as passive
  metadata and long-running Leads spent attention reconciling evidence instead
  of keeping one live owner on the next visible product transition.
- Impact: High agent count, stale-candidate review loops, version proliferation,
  Human wake-up/status work, and strong local confidence without completion of
  the user-visible journey.
- Open question for Lead: What is the one next observable user transition, who
  owns it now, and what concrete emulator evidence will prove it before any new
  audit, release generation, or independent review is opened?
- Recommendation: For prototype convergence, Lead should keep one journey-level
  DoD and one current decision surface. Treat tests, commits, manifests, hashes,
  and reviewer verdicts as supporting evidence only. Staff the smallest owner
  needed for the next visible transition; add review only for material
  uncertainty or irreversible risk. Reconcile every child finish into accept,
  revise, or abandon immediately. Reuse a stable release when a bounded fix can
  do so safely. Preserve compact Human authority and non-secret phase-level
  diagnostics so recovery does not require reconstructing intent or raw payloads.
- Owner decision needed: Starting or resuming the remaining zebra journey is a
  separate operational directive; supervision alone must not implement it.
- Comparable episode count: One audited product journey containing repeated
  instances across Calm, Platform, Animation, and Chief coordination.

### Owner intent altered during Supervisor handoff

- Observation: A request for a real, visually reviewable Personal Pet E2E was
  routed to Lead with an added deterministic-provider constraint and presented
  as a `Project-owner directive`, although the owner had not supplied that
  constraint.
- Evidence: The owner requested Lion source selection, Calm Character review,
  approval-gated animation, installation, and persistence after reload. The
  Supervisor added `Use the current safe isolated deterministic proof path
  unless the owner separately authorizes paid services.` Lead then correctly
  produced a fixed deterministic fixture that could prove wiring but could not
  satisfy visual art review.
- Counterevidence: Avoiding unapproved provider spend was a legitimate concern,
  and Lead faithfully executed the routed instruction with both approval gates
  preserved.
- Suspected mechanism: The Supervisor merged stale workflow context and an
  inferred cost-safety preference into the owner's directive instead of
  preserving request provenance and escalating the unresolved cost authority.
- Impact: The handoff changed the requested output class from a real generated
  character to a mechanical fixture, consumed an E2E cycle, confused directive
  provenance, and returned an artifact the owner never asked to review.
- Open question for Lead: None; the defect originated before Lead received the
  task. Lead should not be made responsible for reconstructing hidden owner
  intent from a mislabeled handoff.
- Recommendation: Supervisor handoffs must preserve the owner's objective,
  output class, constraints, and approval gates. Added context must be labeled
  `Supervisor note` and may not change the outcome. When cost or external-effect
  authority is missing, ask the owner rather than substituting a different proof
  class. Before dispatch, verify that literal execution yields the artifact the
  owner requested.
- Owner decision needed: Explicit authorization is still required before any
  materially costly provider operation; absence of that authorization permits
  escalation, not substitution.
- Comparable episode count: One confirmed cross-role handoff episode.
